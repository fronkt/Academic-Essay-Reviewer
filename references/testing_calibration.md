# Testing Calibration (SAT / ACT / AP)

Used by `academic_context_reader_agent`. All anchors are approximate, shift yearly, and MUST be presented in reports as calibration estimates — never as current-year admitted-class facts. UC files: this entire reference is inert for SAT/ACT (test-blind, `portal_specs.md`) — only the AP sections apply.

## Mid-50 anchors by tier (cross-ref `school_tiers.md`)

| Tier | SAT mid-50 (approx) | ACT (approx) | Reading note |
|------|--------------------|--------------|--------------|
| T10 / hyper-selective | 1500–1580 (MIT ≈ 1520–1570) | 34–36 | Scores clear a bar; they never differentiate. A 1580 buys nothing a 1530 doesn't |
| T20-selective | 1470–1560 | 33–35 | Same clearing logic, slightly wider floor |
| Top publics (OOS) | 1350–1520 | 30–34 | Scores carry more weight in numbers-forward reads (non-UC) |
| Flagship / honors | 1200–1420 (honors/competitive majors: top of range) | 25–31 | Often scholarship- and honors-gate relevant, not just admission |
| UC system | **not considered — test-blind** | — | Grading SAT here is a defect |

STEM-applicant note: at T10/T20 STEM programs the **math section** is read separately — 780+ is the effective expectation for an engineering/CS/math-identity applicant; a 1550 built on 800 EBRW / 750 M reads differently than the reverse.

## Submit-or-withhold logic (test-optional schools only)

1. Score at/above the school's mid-50 **midpoint** → submit. Test-optional pools self-select upward; the published mid-50 is inflated relative to the true applicant pool.
2. Score inside the mid-50 but below midpoint → submit if it corroborates the transcript (strong GPA) or the spike (math section for STEM); withhold if the transcript is the stronger story alone.
3. Below the 25th percentile → withhold, with one exception: under-resourced-school context where the score is exceptional *for the context* (comprehensive-review logic).
4. Never treat test-optional as test-irrelevant; a strong unsubmitted score is a free point left on the table, and readers at required-score schools (MIT, Georgetown-style) get no choice.
5. Report the recommendation as strategy with reasoning, not as a guarantee of how any office weighs it.

## AP / IB calibration

- **Rigor is graded against the school's offerings** (school-report context): 6 APs at a school offering 6 outranks 10 at a school offering 25. The reader must ask for offerings context before judging count; absent it, say the count is uninterpretable and grade provisionally.
- Count norms at full offering access (approx): T10/T20 competitive files typically show 8–12+ APs/IB-HLs by graduation with 5s dominant in-spike; top publics 6–10; flagship/honors 4–8. Anchors, not thresholds.
- **Score-report etiquette**: 5s and 4s → report. 3s → situational (report at flagship tier or off-spike; withhold in-spike at T10 — a 3 in the subject you claim as identity is a contradiction the DA will flag). 1–2 → never report; omission is unremarkable since self-reporting is optional.
- **In-spike scrutiny**: the APs adjacent to the claimed spike are read hardest. A materials-science-identity applicant's Chem and Physics scores are file-load-bearing; their APUSH 4 is furniture.
- Senior-year courses in progress count toward rigor (factor: quality of senior program) — scores obviously pending, list them.
- IB HL ≈ AP for rigor; dual-enrollment college courses count when the transcript context makes their level legible.

## Superscore notes

- SAT superscoring is widespread among selective schools; ACT superscoring less so — per-school check.
- MIT: highest section across dates considered (effective superscore); scores required, so no strategy question exists.
- Report the best defensible composite under the target's stated policy; never average policies across a list.

## Honesty rules

- Anchors above: approximate, drift yearly. Reports say "≈" and "verify against the school's current CDS."
- No invented percentiles, no "your score puts you in the Xth percentile of admits" — that data isn't in the room.
- Testing is one lane: a cleared bar contributes nothing further to the disposition band; a missed bar is a real cost only where scores are required or numbers-forward.
