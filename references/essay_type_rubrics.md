# Essay Type Rubrics

Used by `context_analyst_agent` for classification and by all readers for type-appropriate expectations. Dimension weights guide where each reader spends attention; they are not a scoring formula.

## 1. Common App Personal Statement (650 words)

- **Job**: reveal a person the rest of the file can't show; the one document read by every school on the list.
- **Weights**: voice/authenticity 35%, insight-demonstrated 30%, craft 20%, memorability 15%. Prompt-fit is nearly free (prompts are doors, not fences — #7 is "any topic").
- **Common failures**: résumé-in-prose (taxonomy #10), topic chosen for impressiveness over truth, reflection asserted not shown, adult-retrospective voice (#19).
- **Reader note**: the topic matters less than what the handling reveals; a great essay about a sandwich beats a flat essay about a research award.

## 2. "Why us?" supplement (100–400 words)

- **Job**: prove specific, accurate, reciprocal interest — the school wants to know you see IT, and that it sees a student who'd use what it has.
- **Weights**: specificity 40%, self-to-school linkage 35% (every named resource tied to something the applicant has DONE, not just wants), economy 15%, voice 10%.
- **Common failures**: find-and-replace essay (survives school-name substitution), brochure paraphrase (rankings, "prestigious faculty," campus beauty), all-school-no-self, name-dropping a professor without knowing their actual work.
- **Reader note**: Institutional Reader runs the find-and-replace count; each surviving sentence is a defect.

## 3. Community / identity / background supplement

- **Job**: show the applicant understands their context and what they carry from it into a new community.
- **Weights**: concreteness of the community rendered 35%, the applicant's ROLE in it (agency, not just membership) 35%, bridge to contribution on campus 20%, craft 10%.
- **Common failures**: defining community demographically then saying nothing about lived texture; claiming values the story doesn't show; taxonomy #4 run as template.

## 4. Intellectual vitality / "an idea that excites you"

- **Job**: demonstrate thinking, live on the page. The reader wants to watch a mind move.
- **Weights**: genuine engagement with the IDEA 40% (does the essay contain actual content about it — a claim, a tension, a wrong turn?), idiosyncrasy of angle 25%, connection to the writer's practice 25%, craft 10%.
- **Common failures**: passion asserted with no idea present ("I love how physics explains everything"), Wikipedia-summary of the topic, taxonomy #12's montage.
- **Reader note**: an essay that risks a specific, possibly-wrong claim beats one that admires a field safely.

## 5. Activity essay (150 words) / extracurricular elaboration

- **Job**: add marginal information above the activities-list line — the inside of the activity.
- **Weights**: specificity 40%, marginal value over the list 40%, economy 20%.
- **Common failures**: restating the list entry with adjectives; spending 60 words on setup; taxonomy #5's abstract lesson.

## 6. UC PIQ (350 words, choose 4 of 8)

- **Job**: deliver evidence against the UC's published criteria. An information document, NOT a literary essay.
- **Weights**: evidence-density 45% (what did you do, at what scale, with what result), directness 25%, use-of-opportunity framing 20%, voice 10%.
- **Common failures**: literary scene-setting burying the evidence (a DEFECT here, though a virtue in a personal statement), vague "I helped" claims without scope, choosing 4 PIQs that all show the same strength.
- **Reader note**: anti-pattern #8 — never grade artistry. Portfolio mode should check the four PIQs cover distinct strengths.

## 7. Challenge / adversity essay

- **Job**: demonstrate footing — how the applicant metabolizes difficulty — not to relitigate the difficulty.
- **Weights**: agency and present-tense stability 40%, proportionate reflection 30%, concreteness 20%, craft 10%.
- **Common failures**: taxonomy #8 (trauma dump without arc), blame-shifting (institutional red flag), manufactured adversity (#9 the first B), resolution claimed at a scale the timeline can't support.
- **Reader note**: this type triggers the Institutional Reader's TMI screen; disclosure without control of the material is a discussion-ender in real rooms.

## 8. Scholarship / competition essay (Regeneron STS, Coca-Cola, etc.)

- **Job**: answer the program's actual criteria. For STS: the applicant's OWN scientific thinking, their specific role in the research, and evidence they think like a scientist (doubt, iteration, wrong turns) — not project marketing.
- **Weights (STS-style)**: intellectual honesty & ownership 40%, inside-the-thinking content 30%, clarity 20%, voice 10%.
- **Common failures**: taxonomy #20 (science-fair montage), claiming the mentor's contributions, polish that erases the student's actual voice (juries interview finalists — the essay must survive being compared to the live kid).
- ⚠️ **CERTIFICATION**: STS and most competitions carry authorship rules; Context Analyst arms the banner (SKILL.md Checkpoint 6). For STS specifically the user's own playbook forbids AI-written essay prose — banner is mandatory, every time, no exceptions.

## 9. Additional-information section

- **Not an essay.** If submitted as one, flag it: this section is for context (circumstances, interruptions, explanations) in plain prose. Narrative flourish here reads as misjudgment. Route the review to a fitness-for-purpose check, not the full board.
