# Cliché Taxonomy — Named Arcs, Base Rates, Salvage Conditions

Used by `authenticity_reader_agent`. Base rates are **informed estimates framed as a veteran reader's experience**, not measured statistics — present them that way ("a reader at a T20 sees on the order of ~N of these per season"). Frequency scale: VERY HIGH (several per reading day), HIGH (several per week), MODERATE (weekly), LOW (a few per season).

A cliché topic is not automatically fatal. The finding is CRITICAL only when the essay executes the arc at pool-median quality — i.e., it fails its salvage conditions.

| # | Arc | Shape | Why it fails | Freq | Salvage conditions |
|---|-----|-------|--------------|------|--------------------|
| 1 | Mission trip / voluntourism epiphany | Went somewhere poorer, realized privilege, "they taught me more than I taught them" | Centers the writer's growth on others' hardship; the epiphany is pre-installed | VERY HIGH | Only works inverted: honest about the trip's limits or the writer's specific ongoing obligation — not gratitude tourism |
| 2 | Sports injury comeback | Injury → despair → rehab → stronger person | The arc IS the sport's marketing copy; interchangeable across sports and people | VERY HIGH | The injury must reveal something not about sports (identity outside the team, who they became as a non-player) |
| 3 | Dead grandparent (grief-as-growth) | Loss → sadness → "they would have wanted me to..." | Real grief flattened into a motivation device; reads as using the person | VERY HIGH | A specific, unsentimental relationship rendered in concrete scenes; the essay is about the person, growth stays subtext |
| 4 | Immigrant-family sacrifice | Parents gave up everything → I must succeed → gratitude | The pool at selective schools is FULL of this exact structure, sincerely meant | VERY HIGH | Specificity beyond the template: one particular tension, contradiction, or inheritance — not the general debt |
| 5 | "The true meaning of leadership/teamwork" | Held a title → faced friction → learned lesson stated in the abstract | Lesson is asserted, transferable to any applicant; title does the work | VERY HIGH | The moment of actual cost (what the writer gave up, got wrong, or was changed by), shown not concluded |
| 6 | The Big Game / Big Performance | Buildup → pressure → win (or noble loss) → growth | Outcome-shaped; tells nothing but that the writer competes | HIGH | Only the off-stage angle survives (what happened after, or the part no one watched) |
| 7 | New-kid-in-town | Moved schools → lonely → found people → resilience | Universal experience narrated at universal altitude | HIGH | One granular, unrepeatable scene of the specific strangeness — not the montage |
| 8 | Trauma dump without arc | Serious hardship recounted in detail; reflection thin or absent | Committees are not therapists; without demonstrated present-tense footing, it reads as unprocessed and raises care-flags, not admiration | HIGH | Writer demonstrably in control of the material: distance, agency, and a life being built — the hardship as context, not content |
| 9 | Genius humbled (first B) | Always the smart kid → hit a wall → learned humility/help-seeking | The stakes are invisible to any reader who has seen real adversity in the same stack | HIGH | Honest smallness self-awarely owned, or don't write it |
| 10 | Résumé-in-prose | Tour of accomplishments stitched with transitions | Zero marginal information; the activities list already said it better | VERY HIGH | None — restructure around one thing; this arc has no salvage |
| 11 | Extended-metaphor overreach | Life as chess / baking / running / an equation, sustained across 650 words | The metaphor outruns its accuracy by ¶2 and the person disappears behind it | HIGH | Metaphor as seasoning (an image, dropped when done), never as structure |
| 12 | "Ever since I was young, I loved science" | Childhood wonder → montage of STEM milestones → future doctor/engineer | At research-heavy STEM pools this is the MODAL essay; wonder is claimed, never demonstrated | VERY HIGH (STEM pools) | One real intellectual problem the writer actually got stuck on, and what the stuckness felt like — thinking shown at ground level |
| 13 | The gavel essay (debate/MUN/DECA) | Competition circuit → wins → confidence and worldliness | Activity prestige mistaken for essay content | HIGH | Same as #5: cost, error, or change — not the trophy shelf |
| 14 | COVID essay | Lockdown → disruption → resilience/perspective | The one experience the entire pool shares; differentiation near-impossible | MODERATE (declining) | Only a genuinely idiosyncratic consequence, never the lockdown itself |
| 15 | Quirky-object essay | My sock drawer / my collection of X as a window into me | High risk, high reward: usually quirk-as-performance with no revelation attached | MODERATE | The object must open onto something true and specific; if the object is doing all the work, it fails |
| 16 | Dictionary-definition / famous-quote opener | "Webster's defines perseverance as..." / Gandhi quote | Signals the writer had no opening of their own; drift begins at word one | HIGH | None — cut the opener; the essay starts at ¶2 |
| 17 | Alarm-clock opener | Waking up, morning routine, then the actual topic | Burns 100 words establishing that the writer wakes up | HIGH | None — start where the essay starts |
| 18 | Helping-others-made-me-want-to-help-others | Service activity → circular epiphany → helping career | The reflection is a tautology; motivation is asserted, unexamined | VERY HIGH | An honest account of what the writer GETS from service (discomfort, ego, curiosity) — mixed motives read as human |
| 19 | The 45-year-old's memory | Any topic, narrated with adult-retrospective wisdom and installed insight | Reads as ghostwritten or consultant-shaped; the 17-year-old is absent | HIGH | Re-anchor in what the writer knew and felt AT THE TIME, mistakes intact |
| 20 | Science-fair montage (research kids) | Project → obstacle → poster → award → "research taught me perseverance" | In STS/ISEF-adjacent pools, this is the résumé-in-prose of research; the committee already has the project in the activities list | VERY HIGH (research pools) | The one moment of real intellectual doubt or a wrong turn defended too long — the inside of the thinking, not the arc of the project |

## Reader instructions

- Name the arc by number and title in findings ("Arc #12, 'Ever since I was young...'").
- Quote the sentence where the essay commits to the arc.
- State explicitly whether this instance clears its salvage conditions, and what clearing them would take.
- Two arcs stacked (e.g., #12 + #20) is itself a MAJOR finding: the essay is assembled from templates.
